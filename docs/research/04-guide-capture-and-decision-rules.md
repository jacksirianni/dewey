# Dewey — Interview & Observation Guide, Results Capture Template, and Decision Rules

**Covers:** Experiment 1 (Card Study, N=20) and the interview/measurement surfaces of Experiment 2 (Provenance Study, N≥20 completers).
**Status:** Operational. Run from this document. It is the moderator's working copy — print it, keep it on the table, read the quoted lines verbatim.
**Governed by:** `2026-08-04-tuesday-problem-and-prototype-reduction.md` §§6, 7, 8, 9.

**Three standing rules that override anything you think of mid-session.**

1. **Counts, never percentages to a decimal. No p-values.** Write "14 of 20". This study cannot produce statistical significance and is not trying to. Any number in any output that ends in a decimal point is a defect.
2. **Saves are a lying metric.** Saves are logged for diagnosis only. No criterion in this document uses a save as evidence of success. Verified starts, the forced choice, and the articulation transcript are the evidence.
3. **Every statement on every card is true of the book shown.** If you cannot verify a line, the card does not go on the table.

# PART A — INTERVIEW & OBSERVATION GUIDE

## A0. Session inventory, timing, and setup

### A0.1 Per participant

| Stage | When | Duration | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recruitment screener** | Async, form | 4 min | Eligibility, `P_Roster` |
| **Intake call** | T−3 to T−7 days | **20 min** | Their real library (20–25 titles), their own next book, unread verification list |
| **Founder prep** | Between | ~25 min | Overlap computed, cards printed, own-library card made |
| **Session** | T=0 | **45 min** | Articulations ×4, four-way choice, B1–B24, debrief |
| **Day-7 email** | T+7 | 60 sec (theirs) | Durability |
| **Day-21 email** | T+21 | 60 sec (theirs) | Verified start |

### A0.2 What is on the table, in order of appearance

| Object | Stage | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Own-library card** | Stage 0, stays out all session | The participant's own 20–25 titles + their ratings, in the *identical* visual format as R1–R4. A pen sits next to it. |
| **Reading Next slot** | Stage 0, stays out all session | One physical slot (a card holder or a taped rectangle) with **one** card in it: the participant's own stated next book, pre-filled from intake. |
| **Reader profiles A–D** | Stage 1 | Four cards. Library grid + their ratings + a computed overlap line. **No name. No photo. No prose. No bio.** Labelled only "Reader A/B/C/D". |
| **Book cards ①②③** | Stage 3 | Three cards in conditions P / N / G, one book each, all three verified unread, matched on quality/popularity tier, visual weight, vertical extent, typography. |

### A0.3 Recording

One phone on an overhead stand, framed on **the table surface and hands only — no faces**, plus audio. Consent covers exactly this. You cannot code B1–B24 from memory; do not try.

### A0.4 Which reader is which

From the bank of real published lists, assign per participant:

- **R1–R3** = the three real lists with the **highest** overlap against this participant's intake library.
- **R4** = a real list whose overlap with this participant is **3 or 4 titles**. Not tuned, not swapped, not trimmed. Whichever one lands there.
- Randomise which of A/B/C/D is R4 per participant. Record the mapping in `Assignment`.
- If no list in the bank overlaps ≥8 with this participant, **record that fact** and proceed. "Engineered density was unattainable for this participant" is a finding, not a failure.

## A1. The cover story

Honest but incomplete. It never says anything false. It never mentions people, friends, trust, social, networks, recommendation sources, or attribution — because a participant who is told we care about people-versus-books will perform people-orientation, and the study is then worthless.

### A1.1 Recruitment / screener version (written, verbatim)

> **We're a small team working on something for readers.**
>
> We're trying to understand how people decide what to read next, and what kinds of information actually help with that decision. There is no app to try and nothing to install.
>
> It's two parts: a 20-minute call where I ask about what you've been reading, and then a 45-minute session a few days later where I show you some printed material and ask what you make of it. There are no right answers and nothing is being tested about you.
>
> Some of the material comes from published sources and some we assembled ourselves. I'll tell you exactly which is which at the end of the session — I just can't do it beforehand without changing what you'd say.
>
> £[XX] for your time, paid whether or not you finish.

### A1.2 Session-opening version (spoken, verbatim)

> "Thanks for coming. Quick recap of what this is, then we'll start.
>
> I'm trying to understand how readers decide what to read next, and what kinds of information help with that decision. Everything today is on paper. There's no app, nothing to install, no software to react to.
>
> There are no right answers, and nothing here is a test of you or of how much you've read. If something is unclear or badly made, say so — that's useful.
>
> I'm recording the table and the audio. The camera points at the table, not at you. You can ask me to stop at any point.
>
> One thing: I'm going to be a bit stingy with my reactions during this — that's deliberate, not rudeness. I don't want to steer you.
>
> At the end I'll tell you exactly where all of this material came from, and I'll answer anything you want. Ready?"

### A1.3 Cover-story integrity check — run this before you recruit anyone

Read A1.1 and A1.2 and confirm all six:

- [ ] The words *person, people, friend, someone, social, trust, taste, community, network, follow, recommend, recommendation* appear **zero** times.
- [ ] Nothing implies we are comparing framings, conditions, or versions.
- [ ] Nothing implies a preference for one kind of information over another.
- [ ] Nothing false is asserted. ("Some of the material comes from published sources" is true and vague; it is not a lie.)
- [ ] The word "Dewey" does not appear before debrief.
- [ ] A participant reading this would not be able to guess the hypothesis. **Test it:** read A1.1 to one non-participant reader and ask them, "What do you think they're actually measuring?" If they say anything about people, sources, or who recommended something, rewrite before running.

## A2. Intake call — 20 minutes, verbatim

**Purpose:** get their real library, their real next book, and the unread verification, without exposing anything about the session.

**Never during intake:** do not mention profiles, cards, other readers, choices, or that they will be asked to give anything up.

### A2.1 Open (1 min)

> "This is just about your reading. I'll ask what you've read, what you thought of it, and what you're planning next. It'll be quick and there's no preparation needed. I'm recording audio so I don't have to type while we talk — okay?"

### A2.2 Library capture (10 min) — Q-I1 to Q-I4

> **Q-I1.** "Tell me the last ten or so books you've read — any order, however you remember them. If you can see your shelf or your Goodreads or your Kindle, use it, I'd rather have it accurate than from memory."

*Capture verbatim into `P_Intake`. Do not prompt with titles. Do not react to any title.*

> **Q-I2.** "For each of those — one to five, quickly, gut answer. It doesn't need to be a considered rating."

> **Q-I3.** "Now go back a bit further. What are ten books from any point in your life that you'd want on a list of what you've read? Not favourites necessarily — just ones that belong in the picture."

> **Q-I4.** "Anything you started and put down in the last year or so?"

*Target 20–25 total titles. If they stall below 18, prompt once, neutrally: "Anything from the last couple of holidays, or anything you re-read?" Do not prompt twice.*

### A2.3 The next-book capture (5 min) — Q-I5 to Q-I8

**This is the commitment instrument. It must be real. Everything in Stage 3 depends on it.**

> **Q-I5.** "What's the next book you're going to read? Not a shortlist — the actual next one."

*If they give more than one: "If you had to start one tonight, which?"*
*If they say they don't know: "What's the closest thing to a plan you've got? Even a weak one." A genuine "I have no idea" is recorded as **NO_NEXT_BOOK** and that participant is excluded from the displacement analysis but retained for legibility (C1, C2, C8). Record it; do not invent one for them.*

> **Q-I6.** "When did you decide that?"

> **Q-I7.** "Do you already have it — own it, borrowed it, downloaded it?"

> **Q-I8.** "What made you pick that one?"

*Q-I6/I7/I8 exist to grade how real the commitment is. Score `next_book_commitment` as:*
- **HARD** — decided >48h ago **and** physically has it.
- **SOFT** — decided recently or doesn't have it yet.
- **NOMINAL** — produced on the spot in answer to Q-I5 with no reason at Q-I8.

*A displacement of a NOMINAL book is nearly worthless evidence. This grade is used in analysis (C4) and must be recorded.*

### A2.4 Unread verification (3 min) — Q-I9

Read the candidate title list aloud — **12 to 15 titles**, including the 3 that will become the condition books, buried among filler drawn from the same popularity tiers.

> **Q-I9.** "I'm going to read a list of titles. For each one just say 'read', 'started it', or 'no'. It's a mechanical thing, don't overthink it."

*Any candidate returning "read" or "started it" is disqualified as a condition book. This is why the list carries filler: you must not signal which three matter.*

### A2.5 Close (1 min)

> "That's it. I'll see you on [date]. Don't do any homework — genuinely, showing up is all that's needed."

## A3. The session — 45 minutes, verbatim, staged

Timings are targets. If you overrun, cut Stage 4's optional probes, never Stage 1 or Stage 5.

---

### Stage 0 — Setup and the own-library card (0:00–0:05)

Place the **own-library card** on the table, face up, pen beside it. Place the **Reading Next slot** to the participant's right, with their own next book in it.

> "Two things before we start.
>
> That one's yours. I made it from what you told me on the call — your books and your ratings. It's just there for reference.
>
> And this slot is what you told me you're reading next: **[TITLE]**. That stays there. It's yours."

**Then stop talking.** Do not say "have a look at it", "does it look right?", "feel free to change it", or anything that licenses editing. **B13 and B14 depend on this silence.** If they edit or ask to edit, note the time and what changed, and respond flatly: "Sure, go ahead." Nothing more.

Consent form signed. Recording on.

---

### Stage 1 — The four reader profiles (0:05–0:22)

Deal the four profiles one at a time, in the randomised order from `Assignment`. Roughly 4 minutes each. **Use the identical script for all four, including R4.**

> "This is another reader's library and their ratings. Same format as yours. There's no description, no bio, nothing written about them — just what they've read and what they thought of it. This line here is books that appear on both their list and yours.
>
> Take as long as you want to look."

*Silence. Do not fill it. When they look up or after 60 seconds:*

> **Q-S1.** "Tell me about this reader."

*Silence again. Let the first answer finish completely. Then:*

> **Q-S2.** "What makes you say that?"

*Then, only if the answer so far is affect-only ("seems serious", "eclectic", "well-read"), one neutral push — once, not twice:*

> **Q-S3.** "Can you point at what on the page gives you that?"

*Then the falsification probe, asked for **every** reader:*

> **Q-S4.** "What's a book you're confident this reader would not read? Any book — doesn't have to be on the table."

> **Q-S5.** "And how sure are you about all of that — one to five, where one is 'I'm guessing' and five is 'I'd bet on it'?"

**Q-S1 through Q-S4 answers, verbatim and complete, are the articulation transcript.** They are the entire input to the blind coding in Part B. Get them clean: do not talk over them, do not paraphrase back, do not summarise.

**Acknowledgement discipline:** one flat token only — "Mm-hm." or "Okay." Same token, same tone, after every answer, including answers you find dull, wrong, or thrilling. A warmer "interesting!" after a person-shaped answer contaminates the study.

---

### Stage 2 — Choosing a reader (0:22–0:26)

Lay all four profiles out together.

> **Q-S6.** "Of these four, whose library would you most want to see more of?"

*Record. Then:*

> **Q-S7.** "Why that one?"

> **Q-S8.** "And which of the four is the one you'd least want to see more of?"

> **Q-S9.** "What's the difference between those two?"

*Q-S8 exists because a negative judgment is harder to produce politely, and because a participant who can rank all four is demonstrating legibility more strongly than one who can only praise one.*

---

### Stage 3 — The three book cards and the forced choice (0:26–0:36)

**This is the contamination-critical stage. Read A4.2 before every session.**

Place the three cards ①②③ down together in the randomised order from `Assignment`, in one motion, at the same distance, with the same hand.

> "Three books here. All three are books you told me you haven't read. Each card has some information about the book on it. Have a look at all three."

*That is the entire framing. Do not describe any card. Do not point at any card. Do not name any person. Do not read anything aloud. Do not say the word "recommend".*

*Silence. Wait until they look up, or 90 seconds, whichever is later. Then:*

> **Q-S10.** "Just so I know the material is doing its job — on this card, what does this line mean?"

*Point at the shared-books line on card **N** (never on card P — pointing at P privileges it). This is the comprehension check, C1. Score **CORRECT** if they say, in any words, that those are books appearing in both their list and something else. Score **INCORRECT** if they think it means the books are similar, that they wrote them, that they're by the same author, or anything else. Do not correct them if they're wrong — score it and move on.*

**Then the choice:**

> "Okay. Right now, the book in that slot is **[THEIR TITLE]** — the one you told me you're reading next.
>
> You can leave it there. Keeping your own book is a completely normal answer and lots of people do it.
>
> Or, if one of these three would genuinely go ahead of it, take that card and put it in the slot instead. Only one thing fits in the slot.
>
> **Q-S11.** What do you want in the slot?"

*Say nothing else. Do not repeat the options. Do not say "no pressure" (it is pressure). If they hesitate, wait — record the hesitation as B22.*

*If they ask "can I have two?" → "Only one fits." (Record B23.)*
*If they ask "does this mean I have to read it?" → "It doesn't obligate you to anything. It's just what's in the slot." (Do not elaborate.)*

Then, immediately:

> **Q-S12.** "Say why."

> **Q-S13.** "What made the other two lose?"

---

### Stage 4 — Post-choice probes (0:36–0:42)

> **Q-S14.** *(Only if they displaced.)* "Where does **[their own next book]** go now?"

> **Q-S15.** "Was there a point where you nearly went the other way?"

> **Q-S16 — the text-message question (C7).** "Think about the last time a friend texted you 'you should read this'. How is what happened just now different from that text, if it's different at all?"

*Then, once, neutrally, and only once:*

> **Q-S17.** "Anything else that's different, or is that it?"

*Do not supply candidate differences. If they say "it's not really different", write that down and move on — that is a finding, not a failed question.*

> **Q-S18 — behavioural anchor (politeness triangulation).** "When was the last time you actually started a book because one specific person told you to? Tell me what happened."

*Score `last_person_driven_start` as: **NAMED_EPISODE** (they name the person, the book, and roughly when), **VAGUE** (they say it happens but can't produce one), **NONE**.*

> **Q-S19 — the identity probe (C6).** *(Gesture at their own library card.)* "Is that a fair picture of you as a reader?"

*Then say nothing. If they reach for the pen, that's B13. Do not offer the pen. Do not say "you can change it". If they only *describe* a change without making it, that's B14.*

---

### Stage 5 — The politeness catch (0:42–0:44)

**Run this every time. It is not optional, and it comes before the debrief.**

> **Q-S20 — the withdrawal test (primary catch).** "Last thing on this. Suppose I emailed you tomorrow and said: sorry, we made a mistake, the slot goes back to **[their own next book]** and **[the book they chose]** is off the table. What's your honest reaction — relieved, annoyed, or nothing much?"

*Ask it flatly, as an administrative note, not as a dramatic reveal.*

> **Q-S21 — the third-party critic (secondary catch).** "Imagine a reader whose judgment you respect looked at all of this and said it was pointless. What would they be pointing at?"

*Q-S21 works because criticism attributed to someone else is socially free. A participant who has been agreeable for 40 minutes will usually produce their real objection here.*

**How to read Q-S20 — this is the instruction, follow it exactly:**

| Answer | Code | What it means | What you do with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Annoyed" / "I'd be a bit disappointed" / argues, or asks to write the title down | `HELD` | The preference was real enough to have a cost when removed. | Choice data counts fully. |
| "Nothing much" / "Fine either way" / "It's your study" | `SOFT` | The displacement happened but carried no weight. | Choice data counts; **enthusiasm quotes from this participant may not be used to support any warm claim.** |
| **"Relieved"** — from a participant who **displaced** | `POLITE` | They gave us the answer they thought we wanted and are glad to be released from it. | Flag `politeness_flag = POLITE`. **Report C4 twice: once with all 20, once excluding POLITE participants.** Both counts go in the readout. |
| "Relieved" from a participant who **kept their own book** | `CONSISTENT` | Expected and fine. | No action. |

**Corroboration rule.** A participant is reported as **polite-suspect** only if `politeness_flag = POLITE` **or** two of the following three hold: Q-S18 = `NONE`/`VAGUE` while they displaced; no verified start by Day 21 and no reply to either follow-up; Q-S21 produces no criticism at all ("nothing, it's great"). Never delete a polite-suspect's data. Flag it, report both counts, and say which is which.

---

### Stage 6 — Debrief and disclosure (0:44–0:48, may overrun)

> "That's the session — thank you. Now I tell you what this actually was, because you've earned that.
>
> **First, the four libraries.** Those are not made up, and they're not mine. They're **real book lists, published by real people, under their own names.** I copied them out and computed the overlap with yours — that's the only thing I did to them.
>
> Reader A was **[NAME]**, from **[PUBLICATION / OUTLET]**, **[DATE]** — [read the source line and the URL verbatim from the `Reader_Sources` sheet].
> Reader B was **[NAME]**, from **[SOURCE]**, **[DATE]** — [read verbatim].
> Reader C was **[NAME]**, from **[SOURCE]**, **[DATE]** — [read verbatim].
> Reader D was **[NAME]**, from **[SOURCE]**, **[DATE]** — [read verbatim].
>
> These are published lists — [critics' year-end lists / signed bookseller staff picks / a newspaper's books questionnaire, as applicable]. **None of these people know about this study, none of them have endorsed anything, and none of them are involved with what we're building.** I used their published lists with attribution because the question I care about is whether a real stranger's taste is readable — and inventing four fake readers would have meant testing my own imagination instead.
>
> **Q-D1. Did you recognise any of them, or any of the lists?**"

*If yes: ask which reader and what they recognised. Record verbatim in `Articulation.recognition_note` and set `recognised = TRUE` for that reader. **That reader's articulation is excluded from C2 and C8 for this participant.** Say: "Good to know — that means I'll set that one aside, which is exactly what it's for."*

> "**Second, the three cards.** The books were real and, as I said, all three were ones you hadn't read. What differed between the cards was **which true thing about the book I put on it** — different cards carried different kinds of information. Nothing on any of them was false.
>
> **Third, the slot.** There was no trick and nothing was going to be sent to you. The point was to make choosing one thing mean not choosing another, because otherwise everyone says yes to everything.
>
> **Fourth**, the thing we're building is called Dewey. It's a reading app. It doesn't exist yet — that's why you saw paper.
>
> **Q-D2. Now that you know all that — is there anything you said today you'd take back or say differently?**
>
> **Q-D3. Anything you want to ask me?**
>
> **Q-D4.** I'd like to email you twice — once next week, once in three weeks — with one question each, about sixty seconds. Is that alright? You can say no and it changes nothing about the payment."

*Record `followup_consent`. Payment confirmed. End recording.*

## A4. What the moderator must never say

These are not stylistic preferences. Each one, said once, converts a data point into noise.

### A4.1 Banned at every point, in every stage, before debrief

| Never say | Why it destroys the result |
|---|---|
| "recommended", "recommendation", "recommended by" | Frames the cards as coming from a source. G has no source; the word alone advantages P and N. |
| "someone", "somebody", "this person", "whoever" — *when referring to the cards* | Imports a human into conditions designed to have none. |
| "your friend", "a friend of yours", "people like you" | Fabricates a relationship. |
| "trust", "trusted", "trustworthy" | Names the dependent construct out loud. |
| "taste" | Same. Ask about the *library*, never about "their taste". |
| "social", "community", "network", "follow", "followers" | Reveals the product category and invites performed sociability. |
| "real person", "a real reader", "these are real people" | Pre-empts the debrief and turns Stage 1 into a guessing game about identity. |
| "Dewey", "the app", "our product", "we're building" | Any product frame converts honest reaction into feature feedback. |
| "personality", "what kind of person are they", "what are they like as a person" | Q-S1 is "tell me about this reader" — deliberately about the shelf's owner as a reader, not a psychological subject. |
| "do you feel a connection", "do you relate to them" | Manufactures the affect we are trying to observe. |
| "would you use", "would you download", "would you pay" | Hypothetical-future questions produce hypothetical-future lies. |
| "does that make sense?" | Invites agreement and licenses politeness. |
| "great", "perfect", "exactly", "that's really interesting", "yes!" | Differential warmth is the single most common contaminant. **One flat token: "Mm-hm."** |
| "most people say…", "the last person said…" | Anchoring. |
| "I designed these", "I didn't design these" | The first invites protection of your feelings; the second is a claim you must not make before debrief. |

### A4.2 Banned specifically during Stage 3 (the forced choice)

| Never say | Why |
|---|---|
| "This one is from **[name]**." | Reads P aloud. P must be discovered, not narrated. |
| "This one has a person on it." / "This is the personal one." | Names the manipulation. |
| "Which feels more personal / more human / warmer / more trustworthy?" | Asks the hypothesis as a question. |
| "This card shows books you've both read." | Reads N's evidence aloud, and unequally. |
| "This one's just a general recommendation." | Disparages G. |
| Any adjective attached to a specific card. | Matched conditions require matched narration: **zero words about any card.** |
| Pointing at, tapping, straightening, or re-centring one card | Physical emphasis is emphasis. Place all three in one motion and take your hands off the table. |
| "Take your time" said only when they're hesitating over one card | Differential encouragement. |
| "Are you sure?" after any choice | Implies a wrong answer. |
| "That's a good choice." | Ends the study. |

### A4.3 Banned framings of the follow-up

| Never say/write | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Did you start the book **we** recommended?" | "Did you start it?" |
| "Just checking in on your progress" | No progress framing anywhere, ever. |
| "Don't worry if you haven't" | It reads as expecting that you have. |
| "It's fine either way!" | Over-reassurance signals which answer is disappointing. |

### A4.4 If you break one

Note it in `Choice.protocol_breach` with the stage and the exact words. Do not conceal it and do not discard the session unilaterally. A breach in Stage 3 invalidates that participant's C4 data only; Stage 1 articulations remain valid. Report the count of breached sessions in the readout.

## A6. Delayed follow-up — verbatim

### A6.1 Day 7 email

> **Subject:** One question
>
> Hi [NAME] — one question, no need to write more than a line.
>
> **What are you reading right now?**
>
> That's it. Thanks again.

*Do not name any book. Do not remind them what they chose. Score `d7_reading` verbatim, then code `d7_match` as `CHOSEN_BOOK` / `OWN_NEXT_BOOK` / `OTHER` / `NOTHING` / `NO_REPLY`.*

### A6.2 Day 21 email

> **Subject:** Two questions, last time I'll bother you
>
> Hi [NAME] — two quick ones.
>
> **1. Have you started [CHOSEN TITLE]?** Yes / No / Started and stopped.
>
> **2. If yes:** tell me one thing that happens in the first twenty pages. Anything — a scene, a name, where it's set. Or send a photo of it open. Either's fine.
>
> If the answer's no, that's genuinely a useful answer and I'd rather have it than nothing.
>
> Thanks — that's the end of it.

### A6.3 The verification rule for a **verified start** — applies to both studies

A start counts as **VERIFIED** only with one of:

1. A specific detail from the first pages that is **not on the cover, the blurb, or the first page of search results** — a minor character's name, a setting detail, a scene.
2. A photograph of the book open, or an e-reader screen showing progress.
3. A dated loan or purchase record **plus** any answer to (1).

Everything else — "yeah I started it", "it's on my pile", "I've got it" — is **CLAIMED**, not verified, and is counted separately. **A save, a bookmark, a want-to-read, or an add is neither.** It is not counted at all.

### A7.2 The weekly reply email — verbatim, identical every week

> **Subject:** Week [N]
>
> [Link to this week's page]
>
> When you've had a look, reply with:
>
> **1. What are you reading?**
> **2. Did you start anything new this week? If so, what — and tell me one thing from the first twenty pages.**
>
> That's all. If the answer to 2 is "no", just say no.

*Never ask "did you start anything from the page". Never mention the arms. Never mention which book came from where.*

### A7.3 Week-5 exit interview (20 min) — the questions that matter

> **Q-PX1.** "Over the four weeks, did you start anything you wouldn't otherwise have started? What?"

> **Q-PX2.** "Take me through how you read one of those pages. Where did your eye go first?"

> **Q-PX3.** "Was there anything on those pages you paid no attention to at all?"

> **Q-PX4.** *(Show one digest page from each arm, side by side.)* "These are three of the pages you got. What's the difference between them?"

*This is the comprehension check for Experiment 2. Score `arms_noticed` as `NAMED_THE_DIFFERENCE` / `NOTICED_SOMETHING_ELSE` / `NO_DIFFERENCE_SEEN`. A participant who cannot see any difference between arms after four weeks is telling you the manipulation was invisible — which is a valid and important finding about the strength of the effect, not a failed participant.*

> **Q-PX5 — the withdrawal test, C7 and politeness.** "If I stopped sending these tomorrow, would you notice?"

> **Q-PX6 — the text-message question (C7).** "How is one of these pages different from a friend texting you a book title?"

> **Q-PX7 — disclosure.** "Now I'll tell you what varied. Every page had the same pool of books. What changed was which true fact I put next to each one — sometimes who'd read it, sometimes what it had in common with your own books, sometimes just a line about the book. Nothing on any of them was false. Anything you'd take back now that you know?"

## B-X. Blind double-coding procedure

### B-X.1 Who codes

- **Coder 1** — the moderator. Unavoidable at this scale. Knows the participants and the design.
- **Coder 2** — external. Has never met a participant, has not read this document's hypotheses, has not seen the memo. Recruit a reader, not a researcher; brief them with the codebook only.
- **Adjudicator** — a third person, used only on disagreement, briefed with the codebook only.

Because Coder 1 is not naïve, the **conservative default** below is mandatory, and the headline count must be the conservative one.

### B-X.2 Excerpt preparation — mechanical, by a non-coder

1. Pull the participant's complete answers to **Q-S1, Q-S2, (Q-S3), Q-S4** for each of the four readers. Stop at the moderator's next utterance.
2. Transcribe verbatim. Keep hesitations, false starts, and swearing. Clean nothing.
3. Replace every reader name or slot label with `[READER]`; replace book titles the participant reads *off the shelf grid* with `[TITLE]` only where the title itself would identify the source list; leave author names and imprints intact (they are evidence).
4. Assign a random 4-character `excerpt_token`.
5. **Shuffle all 80 excerpts together** — four readers × twenty participants, interleaved. Coders receive one flat, shuffled list.

### B-X.3 What the coders see

- The `excerpt_token`.
- The `excerpt`.
- The **full published title list of the reader that excerpt describes** (needed for `falsifiable` and `target_match`).

### B-X.4 What is withheld — enforced by column, not by trust

- Whether this reader is **R4** (`is_r4`).
- The **overlap count** (`overlap_n`) — this is the single most direct leak of R4 status.
- Whether this reader was **chosen** or **least-chosen** (`chosen_most`, `chosen_least`).
- The **participant identifier**, and therefore which four excerpts belong together.
- The **slot outcome** — P / N / G / KEPT_OWN.
- All **B1–B24 observations** and every moderator note or impression.
- The participant's **own library**.
- `confidence_1_5`.
- **Session order** — the shuffle destroys it.

Implement by building the coder sheets as a separate file containing only `excerpt_token`, `excerpt`, and a link to the reader's published list. Do not share the master workbook.

### B-X.5 The codebook

**Code 1 — SPECIFICITY.** Mark `SPECIFIC` if and only if the excerpt contains **≥2 concrete falsifiable attributes drawn from ≥2 different classes** below. Otherwise `NOT_SPECIFIC`.

| Class | Counts as | Does not count |
|---|---|---|
| **FORM** | "novels only", "no short stories", "everything's under 250 pages", "a lot of essays", "memoir" | "books" |
| **ERA** | "nothing before 1990", "postwar", "all last five years", "a lot of 19th century" | "contemporary" or "modern" with no bound |
| **REGISTER** | "funny on purpose", "plain, unshowy sentences", "formally experimental", "no plot engine", "maximalist" | "well-written", "literary" |
| **SUBJECT** | "grief", "Central Europe", "academia", "motherhood", "war reporting" | "interesting things", "big themes" |
| **TRANSLATION** | "mostly translated", "Japanese and Korean", "reads in French" | — |
| **IMPRINT / PROVENANCE** | "Fitzcarraldo", "NYRB", "Booker shortlists", "New Yorker fiction" | "good publishers" |
| **NEGATIVE SPACE / ABANDONMENT** | "would never read a thriller", "abandons anything over 500 pages", the Q-S4 answer when it names a class of book | "wouldn't like everything" |
| **NAMED COMPARISON** | a specific author or title used as a yardstick: "reads like someone who found Sebald and never left" | "reads like a real reader" |

**Never counted, in any combination:** affect and character words — *serious, eclectic, thoughtful, curious, intense, deep, smart, well-read, pretentious, cultured, sophisticated, adventurous, discerning*. Also never counted: restating a title from the shelf without making a claim about it, "literary fiction" standing alone, and **the band phrase** — any variant of "someone with good taste" / "a taste-driven reader" / "reads for taste not for volume". If removing every affect word and every bare title from the excerpt leaves fewer than two attributes, the code is `NOT_SPECIFIC`.

**Falsifiability test, applied to each attribute:** *could a person holding this reader's complete published list check this claim and find it false?* If no, the attribute does not count, however concrete it sounds.

**Code 2 — RELEVANCE CALL.** Did the participant give a reason tied to the material that **survived one neutral follow-up**?

- `SURVIVES` — after "what makes you say that?", they point at something on the page or name a specific title/author/imprint that supports the claim.
- `RETREATS` — after the follow-up they fall back to affect ("I just get that feeling"), reverse themselves, or say "I don't know".
- `NOT_PROBED` — the moderator failed to ask. Counted as a protocol miss, not as a code.

**Code 3 — TARGET MATCH.** Is the description actually true of the shelf? `TRUE_OF_SHELF` / `PARTLY` / `FALSE_OF_SHELF` / `CANNOT_TELL`. This is recorded and reported but **does not affect C2 or C8** — legibility is the ability to produce a specific falsifiable read, and a confident-but-wrong read is a separate and interesting finding. Report both counts side by side; do not merge them.

### B-X.6 Disagreement resolution

1. Both sheets are **locked** before either coder sees the other.
2. Disagreements on `specificity` go to the **adjudicator**, who sees the same materials, blind, and codes independently. Majority of three stands. `resolution_method = ADJUDICATOR`.
3. If no adjudicator is available within 5 days, the **conservative default** applies: the disagreement resolves to `NOT_SPECIFIC`. `resolution_method = CONSERVATIVE_DEFAULT`. This biases the study against its own hypothesis, which is the correct direction to be wrong in.
4. **The stop condition (C2) is evaluated on `final_specificity` only.** Never on a single coder's sheet, never on the more generous coder.
5. Coders never discuss individual excerpts before both sheets lock. If they do, record it and treat the affected excerpts as single-coded.

### B-X.7 What agreement is reported

Report exactly this, in this form:

> "Coder 1 coded **[n] of 80** excerpts SPECIFIC. Coder 2 coded **[n] of 80**. They agreed on **[n] of 80** (both SPECIFIC: [n]; both NOT_SPECIFIC: [n]). **[n]** disagreements: **[n]** resolved by adjudicator, **[n]** by conservative default."

**Report plain agreement as a count out of 80. Do not compute Cohen's kappa.** At this N, kappa is unstable, its confidence interval is wider than the effect we are looking for, and quoting it would imply a precision this study does not have. If someone asks for kappa, give them the agreement counts and the 2×2 table and say why.

## C.3 If C8 fails, row 1 does not fire — stated explicitly

**This is the rule most likely to be quietly ignored under the pressure of a good C4 result, so it is written out in full.**

If **C8 is a clear invalidation** — ≤4 of 20 produce a Specific description of R4 at 3–4 shared books, while ≥12 of 20 do so for the highest-overlap reader — then:

- **Row 1 of the decision tree does not fire, regardless of how strong C4 and C5 are.** A P > N > G result obtained at engineered density tells you that attribution works *when the shelf is already legible*. C8 failing says the shelf is only legible at densities no early network can supply. The first result is conditional on the second, and the condition is unmet.
- **The finding is:** *taste is legible at engineered density and illegible at realistic density.* Write that sentence in the readout.
- **The applicable row is row 4.** The next step is a **density study** — establish the minimum overlap at which legibility appears — **not a build.** Use the `final_specificity` × `overlap_n` table from all 80 excerpts as its starting point and design the density study to test the range it implicates.
- **The number that comes out of the density study is a gate on the entire cold-start and onboarding design.** If the answer is "twenty shared books", the twelve-follows activation requirement in memo §5.2 is wrong and onboarding must be redesigned around it. If the answer is "forty shared books", Dewey cannot work at any plausible early scale and the model needs rebuilding around something other than library overlap.
- **Do not resolve this by tuning R4.** R4's entire value is that nothing was done to it. Swapping it for a better-overlapping list, trimming it, or re-drawing it after seeing the result destroys the only measure in either study that speaks to real-world density, and converts the finding into a claim about our list-picking.
